# Opportunity research report format

Aim for about 2,000 words and a compact comparison. Put the evidence that drives the decision in the report and link to the detailed notes. Size the report to the scope and results.

## Decision

Give the date and scope, including any explicit competition restriction in the current brief. Give the first action worth taking and why, and what could reverse the recommendation. State whether anything merits a production build. If zero offers qualify for validation, identify the unresolved question most worth settling. Summarize material eliminations and coverage limits.

## Comparison

Rank up to five offers that qualify under the [decision rubric](./decision-rubric.md) by buyer value, reachable paid demand, and delivery feasibility. Show the offer, launch geography, first user, payer and channel, demand evidence, and expected improvement over the buyer's actual workflow. Give the gate outcomes, dimension ratings and evidence confidence, build eligibility, required versus reachable sales, and the uncertainty most likely to change the decision. If the business case depends on expansion, name the next paid purchase. Give watchlist leads their own unranked list with their missing evidence and next check.

## Field plans

Give plans for up to two eligible leaders. If none qualifies, give up to two watchlist discovery tests. Each discovery test names the missing requirement, the cheapest permissible check, and the result that would address it. Use offline samples or interviews while launch risk remains unresolved. Reserve paid experiments for eligible offers.

For each eligible leader, cover:

- **Offer.** Show the current workflow through success or abandonment, the proposed improvement, and the remaining decisions and checks. Describe the smallest manual service, sample, or prototype that can test it. Where expertise matters, explain how rules, examples, or specialist review would affect delivery.
- **Business case.** Summarize net buyer value, price, reachable volume, contribution, workload, and setup needs. Explain what has to repeat and, where required, how the first sale helps win the next paid workflow. For an intermediary, show why both sides would participate on workable terms.
- **Test.** Start with the cheapest decisive disproof. Specify who to approach, which recent buyer attempt to examine, and which permissible inputs and independently checked outcomes to obtain. When testing performance, compare the offer with the buyer's actual baseline on representative unseen cases, including exceptions. Measure user value, missed errors, review and rework, and delivery costs. Specify a priced commitment appropriate to the model and set thresholds for testing, building, changing direction, or dropping the offer. Tie them to the rubric and the actual economics.

## Reusable findings

Summarize what the evidence says about what buyers pay for, how to reach them, and where manual work or expertise transfer can improve the outcome at a sustainable cost. Include findings that challenge the initial hypotheses and state their scope.

## Alternative format when live research is unavailable

State the access limit and what local evidence you verified. Use `report.md` for a research plan with provisional leads, missing evidence, sources or channels to check, and the results that would change the decision. Keep provisional leads unranked.
